# What the Year 15 numbers mean in context

- **9,485,000 engaged properties** — about **6.5%** of the ~145M US housing units
- **4,300,000 rental doors** — roughly **9%** of the ~47M US rental market
- **5,400,000 homeowner homes** — roughly **6.4%** of ~84M owner-occupied homes
- **158,083 firms** — roughly **23%** of the ~700K US home-service companies. This is the single most aggressive assumption in the model
- **474,250 active professionals** at the 20:1 service ratio

**Commerce and platform fees dominate.** Subscriptions are the smallest of the large lines by Year 15 — they fund the early years and then become a minority of the business. The company ends up monetizing *transactions and data*, not software seats.

## The three assumptions that carry the most risk

1. **Firm recruitment.** 158,000 firms is ~23% of every home-service company in America. Nothing else in the model is as demanding. If firm growth halves, platform fees and subscriptions both halve with it.
2. **Documentation completeness.** Data licensing is a top-three line by Year 10, and every dollar of it depends on records being complete enough to qualify — which depends on pros documenting jobs, which is why documentation is a Power Score factor.
3. **Engagement holding at 95%.** The model assumes the app stays embedded in the move-in process for the whole horizon. If an operator switches platforms, that book of doors leaves at once.

## What is deliberately NOT in these numbers

- Tier 3 data (risk participation / MGA) at $75–300 per record
- Escrow float and payment processing spread
- Agent referral fees and listing-ready reports
- Sponsored placement in renderings
- Patent licensing
- Fast-pay / factoring on commercial receivables
- Any international expansion

Roughly 40 of the 63 identified revenue streams are excluded. **Read this as a floor built on the streams that are understood, not a ceiling.**
