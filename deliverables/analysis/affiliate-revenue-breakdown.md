# PART 4 — SANITY CHECK: WHICH OF THESE ARE SHAKY

Ranked by how likely the number is to be wrong.

### 🔴 Least reliable

**1 · Utility connections ($32.3M by Y7) — the single biggest assumption in the affiliate model.**
The $20/door/yr net assumes ProLnk keeps a meaningful share of a $50–100 bounty. **But Utility Valet is Patrick's company** — he supplies the utility fulfillment, and the channel agreement is unsigned. Depending on how that splits, ProLnk's net could be half this. **This number cannot be trusted until the Patrick agreement is signed.**

**2 · Renters insurance ($17.8M) — legally constrained, not just uncertain.**
Taking a percentage of premium requires a resident producer or agency license in each state. Most states permit an unlicensed referrer only a **flat fee not contingent on premium**. If ProLnk cannot hold licenses (or partner with someone who does), this becomes a much smaller flat-fee stream. **Counsel needed before this is modeled as commission.**

**3 · Rent reporting ($11.6M) — FCRA exposure.**
Furnishing data to credit bureaus makes ProLnk a "furnisher" under FCRA §623: accuracy duties, mandatory 30-day dispute reinvestigation, statutory damages, private right of action. The revenue is real; the compliance cost is not in the model.

### 🟡 Plausible but unproven

**4 · Homeowner furniture & decor ($46.2M — the largest single line).**
$42/home/yr assumes the rendering engine and shop-the-look experience exist and convert. **Neither is built.** This is Phase 2 work that is deferred in the 120-day plan. If the rendering engine slips, this line slips with it.

**5 · Moving companies ($16.2M + $14.3M).**
Straightforward affiliate economics, but assumes 30% of movers book through the app. Nobody has tested that conversion.

### 🟢 Conservative — probably understated

**6 · Storage, security, solar, warranty, homeowner insurance.**
All modeled at low single-digit attach rates. These are the ones most likely to come in higher than shown.

# PART 5 — WHAT THIS SAYS STRATEGICALLY

**Renter revenue leads until Year 5, then homeowner overtakes it.** Renters generate a burst of activity at move-in — utilities, insurance, furniture, movers, all inside a two-week window. Homeowners generate less per event but stay for decades and spend more per household once the shop experience exists.

**The move-in moment is worth roughly $71 per door per year on its own** — which is the entire argument for making the app part of the operator's move-in process rather than an optional download.

**Two dependencies carry $50M of Year 7 revenue between them:** the Patrick/Utility Valet agreement (unsigned) and the rendering engine (unbuilt, deferred). Both are single points of failure on the two largest affiliate lines.
