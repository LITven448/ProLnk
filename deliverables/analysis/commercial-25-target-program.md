# The 25 — Commercial Target Program
**Twenty-five white-label targets, each modeled at $1M+ annual revenue to ProLnk.**
**Researched August 2026. Revenue figures are third-party reported; vendor counts and ProLnk revenue are modeled estimates. Verify before any external use.**

---

## Read this first: 25 targets is a pipeline, not a forecast

Twenty-five signed deals at $1M+ each would be $25M+ ARR. That is the *pipeline* required, not the expected outcome. Realistic enterprise conversion on a curated named-account list is **30–40% over five years — call it 8–12 closed.** The list is sized at 25 precisely so that 10 wins is the base case.

**Qualification bar for inclusion:** to clear $1M/year to ProLnk a company needs roughly **800+ subcontractor firms**, or **150,000+ annual subcontracted work orders**, or **1,500+ locations**. Everything below is sized against that bar.

---

## Why we win — the five things nobody else offers

Use these in every pitch; they are the same regardless of vertical.

1. **The portable credential layer.** A vendor verifies once — license, COI, EPA 608, W-9, safety record — and that credential travels across every network they serve. No competitor has this because no competitor sits across multiple contractors. **It is patent-backed** (ProPass portable credential with site-type gating).
2. **We never take your customers.** Lessen, SMS Assist, and the managed-network players compete by *becoming* the service provider. We are infrastructure. Your brand, your clients, your vendors. Say it in the first meeting: *"They want your customers. We want you to keep them."*
3. **Compliance that physically blocks dispatch.** An expired COI doesn't produce a warning — it makes the vendor un-dispatchable. Liability elimination enforced by software, not by someone remembering to check a folder.
4. **AI triage from a photo.** Issue, trade, severity, and cost band inferred from field photos before a human reads the ticket. Standard FM software routes tickets; it doesn't understand them.
5. **Cross-network vendor supply.** When they have a coverage gap, they can reach verified vendors already credentialed by other networks. That is a benefit only a shared layer can deliver, and it gets stronger with every customer we add.

## The eight profiles worth memorizing

**1 · KBS — the biggest number on the list.** 100,000+ locations serviced daily across 2.9B square feet. At that footprint the vendor network is enormous and almost certainly fragmented. **Pitch:** *"You touch 100,000 locations a day. How many of the vendors doing that work have current insurance on file — today, right now?"* Nobody at that scale can answer, and the question itself sells the vendor registry.

**2 · Ferrandino & Son — the purest fit on the list.** ~253 employees delivering national facility maintenance means essentially everything is subcontracted. They are not a service company with a vendor problem; **they are a vendor network with a company wrapped around it.** Their entire operating model is what we sell. Smaller logo, fastest technical fit, likely fastest close.

**3 · APi Group — the biggest prize and the longest road.** $8.7B, 600+ locations, four service lines, public. Statutorily mandated inspection work means compliance is not optional — it is the product they sell. **Pitch the compliance layer, not dispatch.** Public-company procurement will take four quarters; start now, expect nothing for a year.

**4 · Servpro — a different model, and a supply goldmine.** 2,250 franchises, $4B+ system revenue, Blackstone-owned. You are not managing their subcontractors; you are giving a franchisor a system for its franchise network. Subscription economics work per-franchise. **And every one of those 2,250 units is a local residential contractor** — the crossover value may exceed the contract value.

**5 · Pye-Barker — worst integration debt in America.** 100+ companies acquired in 24 months, 250+ locations, 47 states. **Pitch:** *"You bought 100 companies. How many vendor lists came with them, and where do they live?"* Fire & life safety is compliance-native — NFPA cycles, AHJ requirements, certification tracking — which means the compliance engine *is* the product, not a feature.

**6 · Service Logic — the closing window.** Bain Capital and Mubadala closed in December 2025. A new sponsor writes its value-creation plan in year one and is actively hunting operational wins right now. **Go at the Bain operating partner, not the company.** This window closes and does not reopen.

**7 · Apex Service Partners — the supply play.** 60+ brands, $1B+ revenue, largest residential HVAC consolidator in the country. The contract is worth $1.2–1.8M. **The strategic value is that their technicians are exactly the residential pros your marketplace needs** — thousands of them, in Sun Belt markets, already verified once they are on your platform.

**8 · Contractor Connection / Altimeter — highest crossover on the list.** Insurance managed-repair networks reaching ~99% of the US population, composed almost entirely of residential repair contractors already credentialed for insurance work. Revenue is good; the supply access is better; and it opens the carrier relationships your residential data strategy needs anyway.

## Pitch architecture — one frame, five variants

**The universal frame (four beats, works in every vertical):**
1. **Name their model back to them accurately** — self-perform brand, franchise network, or pure subcontract. Getting this right in the first sentence buys the meeting.
2. **Locate the spreadsheet** — the hand-off from their system to an outside vendor is where software ends. Ask where it lives; do not assert.
3. **Quantify in their language** — DSO and working capital, cost per work order, SLA breach exposure, uninsured-dispatch liability, acquisition integration time. Never features.
4. **Ask for a paid pilot, not a purchase** — $150K, 90 days, one region, their baselines, their metrics.

**Variants by vertical:**
- **Fire & life safety** (Pye-Barker, Summit, Impact, Marmic, Sciens, APi) → *lead with mandated inspection compliance and certification tracking.* Their work is legally required and audited; missed cycles are existential.
- **Restoration** (BELFOR, BluSky, ATI, First Onsite, Servpro) → *lead with surge capacity.* After a hurricane they need 400 verified subs in 72 hours. A pre-credentialed cross-network pool is something no competitor can offer.
- **Refrigeration / mechanical** (CoolSys, Service Logic, Comfort Systems, EMCOR) → *lead with SLA and DSO.* Mission-critical response and faster invoicing.
- **Facilities** (KBS, ABM, Ferrandino, SBM) → *lead with vendor census and compliance coverage* across an enormous, fragmented footprint.
- **Home-services rollups** (Apex, Wrench, Sila, TurnPoint) → *lead with acquisition integration* — onboarding an acquired company's techs and subs in days instead of a quarter.
