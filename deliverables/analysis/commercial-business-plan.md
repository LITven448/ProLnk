# Commercial Division — Business Plan
**White-label dispatch, vendor management, and credentialing for multi-location service companies.**
**August 2026. All projections are models on stated assumptions, not forecasts.**

---

## 1 · The strategic case (this is not just a revenue line)

Commercial white-label pays cash from large solvent companies while the residential platform is still building. That alone justifies it. But the bigger reason is supply.

**Your residential problem:** ~500 pros today. Serving AMH's 60,000 homes needs thousands. Recruiting contractors one at a time is the slowest thing you will ever do.

**The unlock:** you have already solved demand in bulk — operators like AMH deliver 60,000 homes with one signature. **Commercial white-label is the same move on the supply side.** A home-services rollup with 2,000 technicians, a restoration franchisor with 2,250 locations, or an insurance repair network covering 99% of the country — each is one signature that credentials thousands of contractors into your platform.

They pay you to manage their vendors. Those vendors become verified, insured, licensed, and *already onboarded* — sitting in your network when you need residential capacity in Dallas, Phoenix, or Atlanta.

**One relationship, two assets: enterprise revenue and pro supply.**

---

## 2 · Market size

**288 US private-equity roll-up platforms across 32 sectors** are active as of 2026, and add-on acquisition activity currently outpaces new platform formation — meaning the integration problem is at peak intensity right now. Home services alone hosts 30+ active platforms.

Add publicly traded facilities companies, franchise networks, and insurance repair networks, and the realistic universe of companies with this exact pain is **400–600 organizations**. Capturing 20 of them is a $19M ARR business. That is the whole thesis: a narrow, findable, well-funded buyer set.

## 4 · Go-to-market

**The motion, in order:**
1. **Reference customer** — CoolSys pilot produces hard numbers (time-to-dispatch, SLA compliance, days-to-invoice, compliance currency)
2. **Sponsor-led expansion** — one introduction to an Ares, Bain, or ADIA operating partner unlocks a portfolio. PE firms run formal value-creation programs and actively push proven tools across holdings. This is the highest-leverage channel available and it costs nothing but a warm ask.
3. **Vertical proof points** — one named reference per vertical (one refrigeration, one fire & safety, one restoration) makes each subsequent deal in that vertical dramatically easier
4. **Sponsor databases for the long tail** — filter PitchBook/PrivateEquityInfo/Preqin for facilities and building services platforms with 3+ add-ons in 24 months. Add-on velocity is the single best predictor of the spreadsheet problem.

**Sales cycle reality:** 2–4 quarters for Tier A, 1–2 for Tier B. Every deal includes procurement, IT, and a security review. Plan for it; do not be surprised by it.

**Pricing discipline:** every deal must be expressible as tenant configuration. The day one requires a code fork, the economics invert and the product line dies of maintenance.

**Year 1 is profitable from the first customer.** The commercial build costs ~$25K in cash — the dev team is equity-compensated and AI-assisted, so the build is paid in dilution and calendar time rather than payroll. A single $150K design-partner fee covers it six times over. **Do not let a low build cost pull pricing down: price on value delivered, not cost incurred.** The real constraint on this build is not dollars, it is dev-weeks that would otherwise go to the 120-day launch.

**Valuation:** $19.2M ARR at enterprise SaaS multiples of 6–10× revenue = **$115–190M** as a standalone business, independent of the residential platform.

**Upside not in the table:** add-on modules (fast-pay/factoring referral, refrigerant-ESG compliance, parts GPO, predictive maintenance) realistically attach at +15–30% on mature accounts. Mix skewing toward Tier A, or vendor adoption above 40%, pushes Year 5 toward **$28–30M**.

**Downside case:** if vendor subscription adoption lands at 20% instead of 40%, Year 5 revenue drops to roughly **$13M**. Still a good business; plan the hiring against this case, not the base.

## 7 · Quantifying the crossover benefit

This is the number that does not appear in the revenue table and may be worth more than it.

| Channel | Contractors credentialed | Residential-capable |
|---|---|---|
| One home-services rollup (Apex/Sila/Wrench scale) | 1,500–3,000 techs | ~100% |
| One restoration franchisor (Servpro scale) | 2,250 locations | ~100% |
| One insurance repair network | 3,000–5,000 firms | ~100% |
| One commercial refrigeration platform (CoolSys) | 1,200 firms | ~15% |

**Three Segment-B customers could credential 5,000–10,000 residential-capable contractors into your platform.** Against a current network of ~500 pros, that is the difference between serving one operator and serving ten.

Practical constraints, stated honestly: those contractors are contractually bound to their parent, and using them for your residential marketplace requires explicit permission — likely a revenue share or a referral arrangement with the parent, negotiated separately and *not* in the first contract. Raise it after trust is established. But the credential layer, verification, and relationship exist from day one, and that is the hard part.

## 8 · Risks

- **Focus — the actual cost of this build.** At ~$25K cash the dollars are trivial; the binding constraint is dev-weeks. Every one spent on commercial is one off the 120-day residential launch. **Sell now, build after Day 120.** Non-negotiable.
- **Third-party integrations are the estimate risk.** Client FM system connections (ServiceChannel, Corrigo) depend on someone else's API docs, sandbox access, and partner approval — none of which move faster because you code faster. Scope them explicitly and cap how many are included in a pilot.
- **Custom-build creep.** One code fork and the margin structure collapses. Configuration or nothing.
- **Vendor adoption below model.** Mitigate with the license-offset structure so the customer is financially motivated to drive it.
- **Support underpricing.** Mission-critical SLAs mean real 24/7 on-call. Define tiers in writing before signing.
- **Lessen and incumbents.** Lessen has 30,000+ vendors and 3.5M work orders/year. They compete by *becoming* the service provider — your positioning is the opposite and it is defensible: you never take your customer's clients.
- **Sales-cycle cash timing.** Revenue lands 2–4 quarters after cost. Do not hire ahead of signed contracts.
