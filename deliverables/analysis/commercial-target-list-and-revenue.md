# Commercial White-Label — Revenue Per Customer & Target List
**Researched August 2026. Verify all figures directly before quoting them in a proposal.**

---

## What one customer is worth

### Tier A — mega ($1B+ revenue, 100+ locations, 1,000+ subs)
| Stream | Annual |
|---|---|
| Platform license | $300–500K |
| Work-order fees (~45K subbed × $5) | $225K |
| Vendor subscriptions (1,200 × 40% × $149 × 12) | $858K |
| Implementation (year 1 only) | $75–150K |
| **Year 1** | **~$1.5–1.7M** |
| **Recurring after** | **~$1.4M** |

### Tier B — mid ($200M–1B revenue, 20–100 locations, 200–600 subs)
License $150–250K + WO fees $60–100K + vendor subs $215–430K → **~$450–800K/yr**

### Tier C — regional (<$200M, 50–200 subs)
**~$150–300K/yr.** Worth taking only once onboarding is self-service.

### Add-ons that lift any tier
Fast-pay referral revenue (1.5–3% of factored invoices), credential verification, refrigerant/ESG compliance module, parts GPO. Realistically +15–30% on mature accounts.

## Portfolio math — what the line looks like over time

| | Customers | ARR |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 1 pilot → 1 full (CoolSys) | $0.2M → $1.4M |
| Year 2 | 3 | $3–4M |
| Year 3 | 6–8 | $7–12M |
| Year 5 | 15–25 | $25–40M |

**At $25–40M ARR, enterprise SaaS trades at roughly 6–10× revenue — a $150–400M business on its own**, separate from the residential platform. That is the case for taking commercial seriously.

**The honest constraint:** this needs an enterprise sales team from Year 2. Cycles run 2–4 quarters. Years 1–2 are slow and founder-led; the curve only bends once reference customers and sponsor introductions exist.

## TARGET LIST

### Tier A — the best targets

**1 · Pye-Barker Fire & Safety — the single best target on this list**
- **250+ locations, 8,000–9,000 employees, 47 states**
- **100+ companies acquired in the past two years; 22 acquisitions in H1 2026 alone**
- Backed by ADIA / GIC (sovereign wealth); #3–4 on the SDM 100
- **Why they beat CoolSys:** the integration problem is an order of magnitude worse — nobody absorbing 100 companies in 24 months has a unified vendor system. And fire & life safety is *compliance-native*: NFPA inspection cycles, AHJ requirements, certification tracking, mandated re-inspection intervals. Your compliance engine is the whole product there, not a feature.
- Angle: "You bought 100 companies. How many subcontractors do you have, and how many are current on insurance?"

**2 · Service Logic — best timing on the list**
- **$2.2B revenue (2024), 140+ locations, 5,000 technicians**, Charlotte HQ
- **Acquired December 2025 by Bain Capital + Mubadala from Leonard Green (~$3.1B)**
- Self-described largest privately held network of commercial HVAC companies in the US and Canada
- **Why the timing matters:** a brand-new PE owner writes a value-creation plan in the first year. Bain will be actively hunting operational improvements right now. This is the narrowest, highest-value window on the list — and it closes.
- Angle: go at the Bain operating partner, not the company.

**3 · CoolSys** — ~$2B revenue, 110+ branches, 45,000 locations, Ares-owned, 22 acquisitions. Warm relationship. Your live opportunity.

**4 · ABM Industries** *(public, NYSE: ABM)* — very large facilities services, heavy subcontractor use. Public-company procurement is slower; longer path.

**5 · Comfort Systems USA** *(public, NYSE: FIX)* and **6 · EMCOR** *(public, NYSE: EME)* — big mechanical/HVAC platforms. Real targets, but public-company sales cycles and existing internal systems make them Year 2–3.

**7 · Legence** — sponsor-backed building-systems platform, same profile.

### Tier B — adjacent verticals, same exact pain
Every one is a PE-backed rollup with subcontractor coverage gaps:
- **Tecta America** — commercial roofing rollup
- **IES Holdings** *(public)* — electrical
- **BrightView** *(public)* — landscape, huge subcontracted footprint
- **Divisions Inc** — multi-trade facilities
- **Rentokil / Terminix** — pest, enormous route network
- Regional plumbing/mechanical rollups (there are dozens; a PE portfolio-company database is the sourcing tool)

### Know your competition — Lessen (with SMS Assist)
- **30,000+ vetted vendors, 280,000 properties, 3.5M work orders/year, $2B+ valuation**
- They built the vendor network and the platform at scale. Proof the model works — and the most credible competitive threat.
- **But their model is the opposite of yours: Lessen *becomes* the service provider.** They take the client relationship and the work. A contractor adopting Lessen is hiring their own replacement.
- **That is your positioning, and it is strong:** you are the platform that keeps the contractor's brand, their clients, and their vendor relationships — you never compete with your customer. Say it plainly in every pitch: *"Lessen wants your customers. We want you to keep them."*

## Sourcing method for the long tail
The repeatable way to find Tier B targets: PE portfolio directories (PitchBook, PrivateEquityInfo, Preqin) filtered for facilities/building services with 3+ add-on acquisitions in the last 24 months. Add-on velocity is the single best predictor of the spreadsheet problem.

## Sequencing
1. **CoolSys** — warm, in motion, design partner
2. **Service Logic** — go now while Bain's value-creation plan is being written
3. **Pye-Barker** — biggest pain, most compliance-native fit
4. Sponsor introductions from whichever lands first (Ares, Bain, ADIA/GIC portfolios)
5. Tier B once onboarding is repeatable — not before
