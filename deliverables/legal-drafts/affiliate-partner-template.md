## KEY DECISIONS FOR COUNSEL

1. **Scope of rendering manipulation (§3.1)** — how far Platform may alter product imagery (relight, recolor, composite) before it risks misrepresentation of the product or infringes Partner's trade dress expectations; big brands will negotiate this — decide the fallback minimum grant Platform can live with.
2. **AI-visualization disclosure standard (§3.2(b))** — settle the exact "AI visualization / appearance may differ" disclosure and FTC-compliant paid-placement labeling, and whether depicted prices in renders create advertising-accuracy obligations.
3. **Feed license vs. AI training (§4.1)** — the license permits AI processing "to operate the program"; decide whether Platform needs broader rights (training general rendering models on partner imagery) and whether that's negotiable with major retailers.
4. **Attribution and audit asymmetry (§5.2)** — partner-side tracking governing payment is the common retail posture but adverse to Platform; decide the reconciliation threshold and whether to demand server-to-server postbacks as a program requirement.
5. **Marketplace vs. affiliate posture (§8.1)** — confirm the "Platform is not seller/distributor" framing survives how the UX actually presents purchases (especially if checkout ever happens in-app), which affects product-liability, sales-tax marketplace-facilitator, and consumer-protection exposure.
